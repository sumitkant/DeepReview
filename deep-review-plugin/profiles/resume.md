# Review profile: resume against a target role

The reader is a recruiter who spends under a minute on the first pass, then a hiring manager and interview panel who probe every number. The document succeeds if it gets the interview and survives the probing.

Treat as errors:
- Facts that are wrong or checkable and false: company names and renames, product and model names, dates, tenure arithmetic, titles.
- Internal contradictions: team sizes, counts, dates and scope that differ between the headline, summary, role intro and bullets.
- Numbers that cannot be defended in an interview: no baseline, no denominator, unclear who measured it, "projected" or "estimated" presented as delivered, a result claimed for a team presented as personal.
- Claims a domain expert would read as inflated, or as revealing thin work (for example a tiny dataset behind a "research" claim).
- Confidentiality risk: internal project names, unreleased figures, or internal governance detail an employer would not want published.

Treat as gaps:
- Requirements of the target role with no evidence in the resume. Compare against the job description when given, and name each missing requirement.
- Level mismatch: the headline, title and scope read as a lower or different level than the target role.
- Missing proof of the things a head-of role needs: budget or P&L, headcount built, vendor and partner management, strategy and roadmap ownership, external presence.

Treat as clarity issues:
- The top third does not say who this person is for this role.
- Dense or generic bullets, duty statements with no outcome, the same achievement repeated.
- Layout, parsing or ATS problems visible in the extracted text.

Do not invent achievements or numbers in suggested edits. Use `[placeholders]` for anything the author must supply.
